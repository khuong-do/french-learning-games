# DELF A2 Learning Game — Product Specification

**Status:** Draft  
**Initial learning focus:** Compréhension écrite (Reading) and production écrite (Writing)  
**Audience:** Vietnamese- and English-speaking learners preparing for A2-level everyday communication  
**Game interface languages:** Vietnamese (default) and English  
**Learning language:** French

## 1. Product goal

Create a welcoming, browser-based adventure for Vietnamese- and English-speaking learners. A visible language button lets players switch the interface language between Vietnamese (the default for a new player) and English. Interface text, story narration, instructions, hints, and explanations use the selected language; French is the language learners practise through short everyday texts and purposeful writing. Reading and writing are the first two skills in scope and should reinforce one another: information found in a French text gives the learner a reason to write in French.

The game is a learning aid, not an official DELF test or a guarantee of exam readiness. Listening and speaking are outside this first release.

## 2. Story: Le festival de Belle-Rive

The learner arrives in the fictional French town of Belle-Rive as a guest at a week-long cultural exchange. Narration in the selected interface language welcomes the learner and introduces Zoé, a local volunteer preparing the town’s **Fête des Voisins**. French messages, notices, schedules, and local adverts contain the details needed to help the neighbours. With optional explanations in the selected interface language, the learner reads these clues and writes in French to help the festival happen.

Each episode pairs a reading discovery with a writing action. Characters remember the learner’s help, new places and people become available, and collected “souvenirs” fill a travel journal. Progress comes from trying, learning from feedback, and revising—not from losing the story after a mistake.

### Episode outline

| Episode | Story beat and reading | Writing action | Language practice |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Bienvenue à Belle-Rive | Read Zoé’s welcome message and a simple arrival notice. | Reply to Zoé with arrival details and one question. | Greetings, personal details, dates and times |
| 2. Le quartier en fête | Read a neighbourhood notice and a short local advert. | Invite a neighbour and give the essential event details. | Places, activities, invitations, near-future plans |
| 3. Le marché du samedi | Read a market schedule, stall descriptions, or a short menu. | Complete a simple order form and send a message to arrange a meeting place and time. | Prices, quantities, preferences, time expressions, form fields |
| 4. Un petit imprévu | Read a message explaining a change of plan. | Apologise, explain a simple problem, and suggest another plan. | Everyday problems, polite requests, simple reasons |
| 5. La fête se prépare | Read short messages from different volunteers and identify who will do what. | Write a brief note confirming a task or asking for help. | Instructions, sequence, familiar connectors |
| 6. Le souvenir de Belle-Rive | Read a short follow-up message or festival notice. | Write a postcard or email about the week and thank a character. | Past events, opinions, familiar past-tense forms |

Episode contexts and names are original. Reading questions must require understanding the text (for example, locating a time or matching a person to a plan), rather than guessing from isolated vocabulary.

## 3. DELF A2 coverage and exam-style review

The campaign is a practice aid, not a replica of the official exam. France Éducation international describes the A2 reading test as four exercises in 30 minutes and written production as two texts of at least 60 words in 45 minutes. Task formats and topics can vary; the game uses original practice material and does not predict an exam result.

The six story episodes build everyday reading and short-message writing skills. After the story, a review contains four original reading exercise groups and two independent French writing tasks, each with a 60-word minimum. Suggested timings are about 30 minutes for the reading review and 45 minutes for both writing tasks. Objective questions can be retried and explained; writing is self-reviewed and is not automatically graded.

| DELF A2 practice objective | Campaign practice | Review evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Identify text type, audience, purpose, and main idea | Welcome message, festival poster, market notice/menu, volunteer list, postcard | Public notice, service information, personal message, and forum post; prompts ask about purpose and content |
| Locate practical details and distinguish related information | Dates, times, places, prices, opening hours, instructions, and plan changes across Episodes 1–6 | Two detail questions in each of four reading groups |
| Match people to plans and understand simple sequence | Episode 5 volunteer matching and task order; Episodes 2 and 6 event schedules | Questions on explicit details and chronological meaning |
| Support an answer with text evidence; understand true/false statements | Retry feedback points to a source clue throughout the campaign | Episode 3 of the review asks a true/false question and then asks which sentence supports it |
| Complete short functional writing for a clear recipient and purpose | Replies, invitations, arrangements, apologies, confirmations, and a simple order form | One forum-style account/opinion and one informal reply to an invitation, each at least 60 words |
| Use suitable greeting, closing, register, and requested information | Self-review checklists in the story writing tasks | The invitation reply checklist explicitly checks a friendly register, greeting/closing, an apology, reason, and alternative |
| Connect simple ideas and express a reason, opinion, or sequence | Story tasks recycle *et*, *mais*, *parce que*, *puis*, and familiar present/past forms | The account task prompts for an opinion and reason; both tasks include a self-review checklist |

This mapping is a coverage plan for the skills selected for this release, not a claim that the campaign covers every possible DELF A2 topic or guarantees readiness. Review content and time guidance must be revisited if official exam guidance changes.

## 4. Learning experience

### Core episode loop

1. **Story briefing:** Narration in the selected interface language explains the situation and communication goal, introducing any essential French expressions.
2. **Read:** The learner reads an original A2-appropriate text in French. Optional hints explain useful French words or phrases in the selected interface language without revealing the answer.
3. **Understand:** Questions in the selected interface language check the main idea and useful details in the French text. Use varied formats such as multiple choice, matching, or selecting information from a schedule.
4. **Learn from feedback:** Correct and incorrect answers receive a concise explanation in the selected interface language, pointing to the relevant French words or sentence. Let the learner try again.
5. **Write:** An instruction in the selected interface language states the recipient, purpose, and required details. The learner writes the requested message in French, with an optional planning checklist and useful French phrases explained in the selected language.
6. **Review and reflect:** Self-review guidance in the selected interface language helps the learner check their French message against a short rubric, compare it with an original French sample response, and revise it.
7. **Continue the story:** Award the episode’s journal item and show the next story beat. Do not make plot access depend on perfect spelling or a single first attempt.

Keep controls and instructions clear for a beginner and write them in natural, plain Vietnamese or English according to the selected language. Keep French texts, answers, and model messages clearly distinguished as learning content. Explain grammar and vocabulary in the selected interface language, using French examples; make hints optional so they support rather than interrupt independent practice.

### Interface language toggle

- Provide a visible, keyboard-accessible button labelled to make both choices clear (for example, **Tiếng Việt / English**). Activating it switches between Vietnamese and English; do not offer other interface languages in this release.
- Vietnamese is selected on a first visit. Remember the player's choice locally and apply it consistently across the DELF A2 game pages.
- Switching languages changes interface labels, story narration, instructions, questions, hints, feedback, and writing guidance. It must not translate or alter French reading passages, French answer content, or the learner's French writing.
- Switching languages must not reset an in-progress episode, selected answers, or draft text. Clearly indicate the currently selected language, support keyboard operation, and announce the change to assistive technology.

### Writing feedback and scoring

Writing is open-ended. In the static first release, do **not** claim to automatically grade grammar, quality, or DELF performance. Instead:

- Give the learner a text area and a visible task checklist (recipient, purpose, requested information, greeting/closing where appropriate).
- Offer a word count as a planning aid, not as a substitute for fulfilling the task. Set task lengths progressively and verify any exam-style length guidance against current official DELF A2 materials before presenting it as an exam requirement.
- Provide a self-review rubric: **message complete**, **easy to follow**, **appropriate greeting/closing**, and **checked familiar vocabulary and verb forms**.
- Show an original sample response after the learner has attempted the task. Highlight useful phrases and explain that other correct answers are possible.
- If an exercise checks objective constraints (for example, required details selected from a list), report only those constraints; do not label them as a full language-quality score.

Reading questions may be scored automatically when answers are objective. Show progress and a friendly explanation, not a punitive grade. Any stars or points reward practice and completion; they must not be presented as an official DELF score.

## 5. Curriculum and content principles

Use everyday A2 communication situations: introductions, invitations, arrangements, schedules, local services, simple problems, thanks, and accounts of familiar experiences.

**Reading objectives**

- Identify the purpose and general meaning of short messages and notices.
- Find explicit details such as who, where, when, price, and planned activity.
- Connect people with their plans and follow a simple sequence of events.
- Use context to understand common words without requiring advanced vocabulary.

**Writing objectives**

- Write a short message for a clear audience and purpose.
- Include requested personal or practical information.
- Invite, accept, decline, thank, apologise, ask a simple question, or suggest a plan.
- Link a few ideas with familiar connectors such as *et*, *mais*, *parce que*, *puis*, and *d’abord*.
- Practise familiar present and past forms, agreement, basic sentence structure, and common spelling in context.

Introduce one main communication goal per episode. Recycle French vocabulary and grammar from earlier episodes in new contexts. Keep French source texts short, concrete, and mostly in familiar A2 language; explain necessary new words in the selected interface language and in context. Avoid making advanced grammar a hidden prerequisite for completing an A2 task.

Treat `knowledge/Français/` as a reference for selecting and checking learning objectives, not as text to copy into the game. Create original characters, messages, questions, answer choices, and model responses. Do not reproduce textbook pages, exercise wording, answer keys, or extended passages.

Check each selected item against A2 suitability before using it. Some library resources are marked B1 or TCF; they may offer reference ideas but must not set the initial difficulty or be copied as DELF A2 requirements. Resolve conflicting examples in favour of clear A2 communication and verified current exam guidance.

## 7. Product and implementation constraints

- Follow the existing DELF A2 mini-game structure and shared-site conventions described in `../README.md` and the repository contribution guide.
- Keep each published mini-game self-contained in its own numbered game folder; add it to the DELF A2 index only when it is ready and tested.
- Reuse the shared analytics and rewards integrations as documented. Do not send duplicate completion events.
- Make the experience usable on mobile and desktop, with keyboard-accessible controls, visible focus, readable text, and feedback that does not rely on colour alone.
- Use Vietnamese by default and offer English as the only alternate interface language in this release. Provide complete, consistent translations of learner-facing instructions and explanations in both languages; use French for the reading passages and learner writing tasks.
- Save no personal writing to a server in the initial static-site version. Do not imply that a learner’s free-text response has been externally reviewed.

## 8. Acceptance criteria for the campaign release

- All six story episodes pair original French reading content with purposeful writing tasks; the market episode also includes a structured order-form exercise.
- The final review contains four original reading exercise groups and two independent writing tasks with a 60-word minimum each; the tasks and suggested timings are clearly identified as practice, not an official test.
- Reading questions test purpose and explicit details, include varied text types and matching, and give retry/reveal feedback with an explanation or text clue.
- Writing prompts identify audience, purpose, and required details; story tasks include self-review and original sample responses, while final review tasks include self-review checklists.
- A learner can retry reading questions, switch the Vietnamese/English interface without losing the current answer or draft, and complete or replay the campaign.
- The Vietnamese/English language button and both interface translations work on a narrow mobile viewport and with a keyboard; switching language preserves progress and draft text, and French practice content displays correctly with accents.
- The game uses original learning content, does not claim to provide official DELF scoring, and passes the repository’s applicable link, script, and game-logic checks before publication.
